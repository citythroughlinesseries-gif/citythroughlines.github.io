INSIDE THE BOOK

THE CITY IS YOUR CANVAS.

Discover London through an illustrated collection of famous sights and everyday icons. From the engineering of Tower Bridge to the modern forms of the Barbican, each subject becomes a graphic composition with open spaces for creative colouring.

Designed for adults who enjoy travel, architecture and quiet creative moments, the book invites you to slow down and see familiar places in a different way.

A GALLERY OF LINES

17 LONDON ICONS.
17 STORIES TO COLOUR.

Get to know the places and details that inspired the illustrations.

01

Big Ben

Completed in 1859, Big Ben is the famous bell in the Elizabeth Tower and one of London’s most recognizable symbols.

02

The Gherkin

Completed in 2003, the Gherkin is a distinctive glass skyscraper famous for its curved shape and modern skyline presence.

03

Tower Bridge

Opened in 1894, Tower Bridge is London’s iconic bascule bridge, famous for its twin towers and liftable roadway.

04

Double-Decker Bus

Introduced in London in the early 20th century, the red double-decker bus is a classic and practical symbol of the city.

05

Barbican Centre

Opened in 1982, the Barbican Centre is a major arts complex famous for its Brutalist design and cultural programme.

06

Old Spitalfields Market

Rebuilt in its modern form in 2005, Old Spitalfields Market is famous for its mix of food, fashion, and independent stalls.

07

London Eye

Opened in 2000, the London Eye is famous for its giant observation wheel and panoramic views over the city.

08

Red Telephone Box

First introduced in the 1920s, the red telephone box is one of Britain’s most recognizable design icons.

09

Hyde Park

Established as a royal hunting ground in 1536 by Henry VIII and opened to the public in 1637, Hyde Park is famous for its vast green spaces, Serpentine lake, and major outdoor concerts.

10

Kew Gardens

Founded in 1759, Kew Gardens is famous worldwide for its plant collections, glasshouses, and scientific importance.

11

Tate Modern

Opened in 2000, Tate Modern is famous for its landmark turbine hall, modern art collection, and riverside setting.

12

Borough Market

Dating back to the 12th century, Borough Market is famous as one of London’s oldest and best-known food markets.

13

Soho

Developed over centuries, Soho is famous for nightlife, restaurants, creative energy, and its lively central location.

14

Shoreditch

Once an industrial district, Shoreditch is now famous for street art, startups, and its creative scene.

15

The Shard

Completed in 2012, The Shard is famous for being London’s tallest building and for its sharp glass spire design.

16

Brick Lane

Opened in 2007 inside the historic Old Truman Brewery, Rough Trade East is famous for vinyl, live in-store sessions, and independent music culture, while the Truman Brewery site is known as a major creative and market hub in East London.

17

Columbia Road Flower Market

Established in 1976, Columbia Road Flower Market is famous for its vibrant Sunday blooms, lively East London atmosphere, and independent shops.
