A GALLERY OF LINES

17 SEOUL ICONS.
17 STORIES TO COLOUR.

Discover the places and details that inspired the illustrations in this edition.

01

Gwanghwamun Gate

Built in 1395, Gwanghwamun is the grand main gate of Gyeongbokgung Palace and one of Seoul’s most recognisable historic landmarks.

02

Banpo Bridge & Rainbow Fountain

Spanning the Han River, Banpo Bridge is famous for its Moonlight Rainbow Fountain, a spectacular display of water, colourful lights and music.

03

Ihwa Mural Village

Transformed by a public art project in 2006, Ihwa Mural Village is known for its hillside streets, murals, sculptures and views across Seoul.

04

N Seoul Tower

Rising above Namsan Mountain, N Seoul Tower is a distinctive city landmark offering panoramic views across the Seoul skyline.

05

Lotte World Tower

Standing 555 metres tall, Lotte World Tower is South Korea’s tallest building and a striking feature of Seoul’s modern skyline.

06

Haechi

Haechi is Seoul’s mythical guardian creature, traditionally associated with justice, safety and protection, and adopted as a symbol of the city.

07

Sungnyemun (Namdaemun Gate)

Completed in 1398, Sungnyemun was one of the main gates of the historic walled city and is a treasured landmark of Joseon-era Seoul.

08

Han River & Seoul Skyline

Flowing through Seoul, the Han River is framed by parks, bridges and a modern skyline, forming one of the defining landscapes of the city.

09

Cheonggyecheon Stream

Running through central Seoul, Cheonggyecheon is a restored urban stream and public space that provides a peaceful contrast to the surrounding city streets.

10

Gwangjang Market

Established in 1905, Gwangjang Market was Korea’s first permanent market and is now celebrated for street food, textiles, hanbok and traditional market culture.

11

Jogyesa Temple & Lotus Lanterns

A major centre of Korean Buddhism, Jogyesa is the head temple of the Jogye Order and is beautifully decorated with lanterns for celebrations surrounding Buddha’s Birthday.

12

Bungeoppang

Bungeoppang is a beloved Korean street snack made from fish-shaped batter, traditionally filled with sweet red bean paste and especially popular in winter.

13

Ikseon-Dong Hanok Alley

Developed as a hanok neighbourhood in the 1920s, Ikseon-dong is known today for its narrow lanes, traditional houses, cafés, restaurants and creative shops.

14

Seoul Street Signs & Hangul

Hangul, Korea’s distinctive writing system, gives Seoul’s street signs, shopfronts and neighbourhoods an instantly recognisable visual character.

15

Changdeokgung Secret Garden

Created as part of the royal palace grounds, Changdeokgung’s Secret Garden is celebrated for its pavilions, ponds and landscape designed in harmony with the natural terrain.

16

Seoul Subway Train

Seoul’s extensive subway system connects neighbourhoods across the capital and has become an essential part of everyday life in this fast-moving city.

17

Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP)

Opened in 2014 and designed by architect Zaha Hadid, Dongdaemun Design Plaza is renowned for its sweeping curves and futuristic architecture.
